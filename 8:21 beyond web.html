More Than a Diagnosis

不止于一个诊断

Autism, or Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), is a neurodevelopmental condition that affects how a person communicates, interacts, and experiences the world. Children with autism may see, hear, and feel things differently — and that difference is not a deficit, but a different way of being.

自闭症（孤独症谱系障碍，ASD）是一种神经发育性疾病，影响着一个人的沟通方式、社交互动和对世界的感知。自闭症孩子可能以不同的方式去看、去听、去感受——这种不同不是缺陷，而是一种独特的存在方式。

Our purpose is not only to provide financial support for their daily lives and special needs —

it is to be there, to sit beside them, and to accompany them.

我们的目的不仅仅是用金钱支持他们的日常生活和特殊需求——更重要的是，我们需要陪伴他们。

Our Activities

Dialogue Club • 对白社

每两周一次的温暖相聚

Every other Sunday, we join the Dialogue Club (对白社) — a creative drama workshop where we spend time with children with autism through storytelling and performance.

每隔一周的星期日，我们参加"对白社"活动——这是一个通过故事和表演与自闭症孩子们共度时光的创意戏剧工作坊。

1

A Theme Begins

一个主题的开始

Each session starts with a new theme — a story, an emotion, or an adventure waiting to unfold.

每次活动都有一个新主题——一个故事、一种情感，或一段等待展开的冒险。

2

Small Groups Form

分组合作

Everyone splits into small groups, each with one or two children with autism, guided by volunteers.

大家分成几个小组，每组有一到两位自闭症小朋友，由志愿者陪伴引导。

3

Rehearse & Create

排练与创作

Over one to two hours, each group writes and rehearses a short script together — turning ideas into a performance.

花一到两个小时，每组一起编写并排练一个短剧本——把创意变成一场表演。

4

Shine & Cheer

闪耀与欢呼

Every group takes the stage to perform. We cheer, we clap, we celebrate each other — because every voice deserves to be heard.

每个组轮流上台展示。我们为彼此欢呼、鼓掌、祝贺——因为每一个声音都值得被听见。

🧹 HUDDLE TIME

Join Beyond

Become part of something bigger than yourself.

成为比自己更伟大的事物的一部分

When you join Beyond, you'll spend our noon recess club time knitting scarves, making crafts, and learning about autism together. The atmosphere is relaxed, warm, and full of laughter — we call each gathering a Huddle, and the time we share, Huddle Time.

加入 Beyond 后，我们会在中午 recess 社团时间一起织围巾、做手工，同时了解自闭症。整个社团氛围轻松温暖、充满笑声——我们把每次聚在一起玩或做事的时光称为 Huddle / Huddle Time。

All handmade crafts are sold at special festivals, charity events, and school sales — and every yuan raised goes directly to supporting children with autism.

所有手工艺品会在特殊节日、公益活动及义卖时售出，所得款项全部用于支持自闭症小朋友。

Metaphor's Crack

隐喻的裂隙

We talk in hints, jokes, and polite lies. But if you take every word literally, these everyday phrases become walls instead of bridges.

我们说话总是带暗示、开玩笑、说客套话。但如果有人把每句话都按字面意思理解，这些日常用语就变成了墙，而不是桥。

Source: artwithmisslinda.com · Shattered Glass Art

🔍 When "I'm fine" doesn't mean fine

当"我没事"不是真的没事

When someone says "I'm fine," they usually mean the opposite. When they say "Could you pass the salt?" they're not asking about your ability — they just want the salt. Most people pick up these hidden meanings without thinking. But for someone with autism, words often mean exactly what they say. This leads to misunderstandings that seem small to us but feel huge to them.

当有人说"我没事"的时候，往往意思正好相反。当有人说"你能把盐递给我吗？"，不是在问你有没有能力，只是想让你递个盐。大多数人不用想就能听出这些隐藏的意思。但对自闭症人士来说，话就是字面上的意思。这会导致一些我们觉得无所谓、但对他们来说很大的误会。

💬 Polite words that confuse

让人困惑的客套话

"Let's grab coffee sometime!" "We should hang out!" Most of the time, these are just nice things people say — not real invitations. But if you take them literally, you might wait for a call that never comes. Over time, this makes social life confusing and exhausting for people with autism. It's not that they don't want to connect — it's that the rules keep changing.

"有空一起喝咖啡！""改天约！"大多数时候，这些只是客套话，不是真的约你。但如果按字面理解，你可能会一直等一个永远不会来的电话。时间久了，社交对自闭症人士来说变得又累又困惑。不是他们不想交朋友，而是规则一直在变。

🎭 Idioms that make no sense

毫无道理的成语和俗语

"Break a leg" means good luck. "Spill the beans" means tell a secret. "Hit the books" means study hard. If you read these word by word, they make zero sense. People with autism aren't less smart — they just understand language in a more direct way. The problem isn't them. It's that our language is full of hidden codes they were never given the key to.

"Break a leg"是祝你好运。"Spill the beans"是泄露秘密。"Hit the books"是用功读书。如果逐字翻译，这些短语根本说不通。自闭症人士不是不聪明——他们只是用更直接的方式理解语言。问题不在他们，而在于我们的语言里藏着太多他们没有钥匙的密码。

"I think in pictures. Words are like a second language to me."

"我用画面思考。文字对我来说就像第二语言。"

— Temple Grandin, autism advocate & professor

For someone who thinks in pictures, phrases full of hidden meanings are like a foreign language nobody taught them. The "crack" in communication isn't because they're not listening — it's because we're speaking in code.

对一个用画面思考的人来说，充满暗示的话语就像一门没人教过的外语。沟通中的"裂隙"不是因为他们没在听，而是因为我们在用密码说话。

Object-Mediator

物件的代理者

Sometimes you can't say what you feel with words. An object — a toy, a pebble, a string of beads — can say it for you.

有时候用嘴说不出来的感受，一个小东西——一个玩具、一块石头、一串珠子——可以替你说出来。

Source: freepik.com · Stacked Aesthetic Objects Still Life

🧸 More than "just a toy"

不只是"一个玩具"

For some autistic kids, a favorite object isn't just a thing they carry around. It helps them feel calm when everything else feels too loud, too bright, or too fast. It's like a security blanket — but one that speaks a language only they fully understand.

对一些自闭症孩子来说，一个心爱的小物件不只是随身带着的东西。当周围的一切都太吵、太亮、太快的时候，它能帮他们安静下来。就像一条安心毯——但只有他们自己听得懂它在说什么。

🔄 Why they line things up

为什么他们喜欢把东西排成一排

You might have seen an autistic child line up toys in a perfect row, spin a wheel over and over, or arrange objects by color. Some people think this is "weird." But think about it — when you're stressed, don't you also do something repetitive? Tap your pen, bounce your leg, crack your knuckles? For autistic kids, repetition isn't random. It's how they make a chaotic world feel orderly.

你可能见过自闭症孩子把玩具排成一排，反反复复转一个轮子，或者按颜色摆东西。有些人觉得这是"奇怪的行为"。但想一想——你紧张的时候不也会做些重复的动作吗？转笔、抖腿、掰手指？对自闭症孩子来说，重复不是没意义的。这是他们在混乱的世界里给自己建立秩序的方式。

🌉 Objects as messengers

物件是信使

When a child hands you a toy, they might not be asking you to play. They might be saying: "This is how I feel." "This is what I need right now." The object becomes a messenger — carrying feelings that words can't. If we only see "a kid playing with toys," we miss the whole conversation.

当一个孩子递给你一个玩具，他可能不是想让你陪他玩。他可能在说："这是我的感受。""这是我需要的。"物件成了信使，传递着语言说不清楚的东西。如果我们只看到"一个在玩玩具的孩子"，我们就错过了整段对话。

"Different, not less."

"是不同，不是更差。"

— Temple Grandin

When an autistic child lines up toys or holds onto a pebble, they're not doing something "less." They're doing something different — communicating in a way we're only starting to understand. The object isn't a wall between us. It's the bridge.

当自闭症孩子排列玩具或紧握一颗石子时，他们不是在"退步"。他们是在用不同的方式沟通——一种我们才刚开始理解的方式。物件不是挡在我们中间的墙，而是连接我们的桥。

Weaving Mirror

织作的镜像

We knit scarves to raise money for autistic kids. But sitting there, looping yarn row after row, we started to see ourselves in them.

我们织围巾义卖，是为了帮助自闭症孩子。但坐在那里，一针一针绕着毛线，我们开始在他们身上看到了自己。

Source: sarahmaker.com · Side Line Scarf Knitting

🧶 Our scarf project

我们的围巾项目

Every scarf Beyond sells starts with a single loop of yarn. We sit together during lunch break, needles clicking, yarn going round and round. Some of us count every stitch. Others just let their hands find the rhythm. It looks simple — but there's something calming about doing the same thing over and over.

Beyond 卖出的每一条围巾都从一圈毛线开始。午休的时候我们坐在一起，棒针交错，毛线一圈又一圈。有人一针一针地数，有人让手自己找到节奏。看起来很简单——但一遍遍做同样的事，有一种让人安静下来的力量。

🔄 We're not so different

我们其实没那么不同

Knitting is all about repetition: the same stitch, the same motion, over and over. Sound familiar? This is exactly what many autistic kids do — line things up, repeat actions, find comfort in patterns. We used to see that as "their behavior." Now, sitting here knitting, we realized: we do the same thing. We just call it "hobby" instead of "symptom."

编织就是重复：同样的针法、同样的动作，一遍又一遍。听起来熟悉吗？这正是很多自闭症孩子在做的事——排列东西、重复动作、在规律中找到安心。我们以前觉得那是"他们的行为"。现在坐在那里织围巾，我们发现：我们也在做一样的事。只是我们管它叫"爱好"，不叫"症状"。

"In every stitch, I find something I didn't expect — not just a scarf, but a quiet conversation between my hands and my mind."

"每一针里，我都找到了意料之外的东西——不只是围巾，更是一场手和心之间的安静对话。"

🪞 The mirror turned around

镜子转向了自己

We started this project to help autistic children. But somewhere between the first stitch and the last, the mirror turned around. The focus, the repetition, the need for order — these aren't "autistic traits." They're human traits. Weaving didn't just make scarves. It made us understand.

我们开始这个项目是为了帮助自闭症孩子。但在第一针和最后一针之间的某个时刻，镜子转向了我们自己。那份专注、那份重复、对秩序的需要——这些不是"自闭症的特质"，而是人的特质。编织不只是做出了围巾，也让我们真正理解了。

"The world needs different kinds of minds to work together."

"这个世界需要不同的头脑一起合作。"

— Temple Grandin

Every row of knitting taught us that the line between "us" and "them" is much thinner than we thought. The repetition we used to see as a "symptom" became something we experienced ourselves. Different minds aren't a problem to fix — they're the reason the world works.

编织的每一行都在告诉我们：我们和"他们"之间的界限，比想象中薄得多。那些我们曾经当作"症状"的重复行为，变成了我们自己正在经历的事。不同的头脑不是需要解决的问题，而是这个世界运转的原因。

Parallel Hypothesis

平行的可能性

"Normal" is just a set of rules that most people agreed on. But what if the rules were different? Who would be "normal" then?

"正常"只不过是大多数人商量好的一套规则。如果规则变了，谁才是"正常"的？

Source: Wixstatic.com · Parallel Universe Art

🌌 What if the world were different?

如果世界是另一个样子呢？

Imagine a world where saying exactly what you think is considered polite. Where "I don't want to" is better than "I'd love to, but I'm busy." Where rocking back and forth is seen as dancing, not a symptom. Where lining up objects is an art form. In this world, who would need "social skills training" — autistic people, or everyone else?

想象一个这样的世界：想到什么就说什么被认为是礼貌的。"我不想"比"我很想去但没空"更得体。来回摇晃身体被看作跳舞，而不是症状。把东西排成一行是一种艺术。在这个世界里，谁需要"社交技能训练"——是自闭症人士，还是其他人？

⚖️ Who made these rules?

这些规则是谁定的？

We say eye contact is "normal." Small talk is "necessary." Lining up toys is "weird." But who decided this? These aren't laws of nature — they're just things the majority got used to. If 90% of people were autistic, the rules would flip: eye contact might seem rude, and "How are you?" would be a strange question because nobody actually wants the real answer.

我们说眼神交流是"正常的"。寒暄是"必要的"。排列玩具是"奇怪的"。但谁定的？这些不是自然法则——只是大多数人习惯了的东西。如果 90% 的人是自闭症，规则就会反过来：盯着人看可能被认为没礼貌，"你好吗？"会变成一个奇怪的问题——因为没人真的想知道答案。

🤔 Think about it:

If 90% of people were autistic, what would "social skills training" look like?
If loud sounds bothered everyone, would fireworks be banned?
If everyone loved talking about their favorite topic for hours, would the world have more ideas — or just louder conversations?

如果 90% 的人是自闭症，"社交训练"会教什么？如果所有人都怕吵，烟花会被禁吗？如果每个人都喜欢聊自己喜欢的话题聊上几小时，世界会有更多想法——还是只是更吵？

🔄 The point

所以呢？

This isn't just a daydream. It's a way to understand. When you imagine a world where the rules are flipped, you start to see that our rules are just one option — not the only option. We don't need to pretend autism doesn't exist. We just need to stop pretending our way is the only way.

这不只是白日梦。这是一种理解的方式。当你想象一个规则翻转的世界，你就会发现：我们的规则只是一种选择，不是唯一的选择。我们不需要假装自闭症不存在，只需要停止假装我们的方式是唯一的方式。

"If you've met one person with autism, you've met one person with autism."

"如果你遇见过一个自闭症人士，那你只遇见过一个自闭症人士。"

— Dr. Stephen Shore, autism advocate & professor

No two autistic people are the same. So maybe there's no such thing as "normal" — just lots of different ways to be human. The question isn't "how do we fix the people who are different." It's "how do we build a world where everyone fits."

没有两个自闭症人士是一样的。所以也许根本不存在"正常"——只有很多种做人的方式。问题不是"怎么修正那些不一样的人"，而是"怎么建一个让所有人都能待下去的世界"。

Our Tradition

我们的传统

At every fair, we don't only sell ready-made items — we also take custom orders on the spot. A customer picks their favorite color, shape, and product, then draws a little self-portrait and writes their name on our whiteboard. We make it, and they come back at the agreed time to pick it up.

在每一次集市上，我们不仅卖现成的手作，还接受现场定制。客人选好自己喜欢的颜色、版型和制品，然后在我们的白板上画一幅小肖像、写下名字。我们做好后，他们按约定时间来取。
